Chapter 9: Trigonometry

Nine lessons that take you from right-triangle ratios to general angles, transformed graphs, trigonometric equations and identity proofs.

From right triangles to the whole circle

Trigonometry begins with the ratios in a right-angled triangle, but the subject only becomes powerful once you let the angle grow beyond 90°.

Trigonometry is the mathematics of angles and periodic change. In this chapter you start with the familiar sine, cosine and tangent ratios of acute angles, then extend those definitions to angles of any size using the unit circle. That single shift is what makes it possible to describe waves, oscillations and rotation — and to solve equations that come up again and again in physics, engineering and calculus.

The lessons are arranged in a deliberate order. Sections 9.1 to 9.3 rebuild the foundations: exact values, the general definition of an angle, and the signs of the ratios in each quadrant. Sections 9.4 and 9.5 turn those definitions into pictures, so you can sketch y = sin x, y = cos x and y = tan x and their modulus forms without memorising every point.

Sections 9.6 to 9.9 are where trigonometry becomes an algebraic tool. You will solve trigonometric equations over a stated interval, prove identities using the Pythagorean and reciprocal relationships, and finally handle double-angle formulae and more involved proofs. Each section includes worked examples with every line explained, followed by practice problems that check your answer immediately.

If you are revising for an exam, work through the sections in order the first time, then use the cards below to jump straight back to whichever technique you need to refresh.

What you will learn

  • Exact values of sin, cos and tan for 30°, 45° and 60°
  • Angles in standard position, reference angles and coterminal angles
  • Signs of the ratios in the four quadrants, and the graphs of y = sin x, y = cos x and y = tan x
  • Modulus graphs such as y = |sin x|
  • Solving equations like sin θ = k over a given interval
  • The Pythagorean identities: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  • Double-angle formulae for sin 2θ and cos 2θ

Prerequisite: confident algebra, solving linear and quadratic equations, and the basic SOHCAHTOA ratios from earlier years.
