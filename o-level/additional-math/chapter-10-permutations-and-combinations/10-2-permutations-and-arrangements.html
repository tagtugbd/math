10.2: Permutations and Arrangements

Solving arrangement problems with restrictions using factorials

Learn how to calculate the number of ways to arrange items in a row. This lesson covers basic permutations, arrangements with repeated letters, restrictions on positions (like 'even' or 'greater than'), and the 'block' method for keeping items together.

1. Find the number of different arrangements of

1a. 4 people sitting in a row on a bench

We need to arrange 4 distinct people in 4 positions.
Number of arrangements = 4!
\[ = 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 24 \]

1b. 7 different books on a shelf

We need to arrange 7 distinct books in 7 positions.
Number of arrangements = 7!
= 5040

2. Find the number of different arrangements of letters in each of the following words.

2a. TIGER

The word TIGER has 5 distinct letters.
Number of arrangements = 5!
= 120

2b. OLYMPICS

The word OLYMPICS has 8 distinct letters.
Number of arrangements = 8!
= 40320

2c. PAINTBRUSH

The word PAINTBRUSH has 10 distinct letters.
Number of arrangements = 10!
= 3628800

3. Four-digit numbers using 3, 5, 7 and 8 without repetition.

3a. Find the number of different four-digit numbers that can be formed.

We are arranging 4 distinct digits into 4 slots.
Number of ways = 4!
= 24

3b-i. How many of these four-digit numbers are even?

For the number to be even, the last digit must be 8 (the only even digit).
Last digit: 1 choice (8).
The remaining 3 digits can be arranged in the first 3 slots in 3! ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 1 \times 3! = 6 \]

3b-ii. How many of these four-digit numbers are greater than 8000?

For the number to be greater than 8000, the first digit must be 8.
First digit: 1 choice (8).
The remaining 3 digits can be arranged in the last 3 slots in 3! ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 1 \times 3! = 6 \]

4. A shelf holds 7 different books. Four of the books are cookery books and three of the books are history books.

4a. Find the number of ways the books can be arranged if there are no restrictions.

We are arranging 7 distinct books in 7 slots.
Number of ways = 7!
= 5040

4b. Find the number of ways the books can be arranged if the 4 cookery books are kept together.

Treat the 4 cookery books as a single 'block'.
Now we have 1 block + 3 history books = 4 items to arrange.
These 4 items can be arranged in 4! ways.
Within the 'block', the 4 cookery books can be arranged in 4! ways.
\[ \text{Total arrangements} = 4! \times 4! = 24 \times 24 = 576 \]

5. Five-digit numbers are to be formed using the digits 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Each digit may be used only once in any number.

5a. Find how many different five-digit numbers can be formed.

We are arranging 5 distinct digits in 5 slots.
Number of ways = 5!
= 120

5b. How many of these five-digit numbers are even?

For the number to be even, the last digit must be 2, 4, or 6.
Last digit: 3 choices.
The remaining 4 digits can be arranged in 4! ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 3 \times 4! = 3 \times 24 = 72 \]

5c. How many of these five-digit numbers are greater than 40000?

For the number to be greater than 40000, the first digit must be 4, 5, or 6.
First digit: 3 choices.
The remaining 4 digits can be arranged in 4! ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 3 \times 4! = 3 \times 24 = 72 \]

5d. How many of these five-digit numbers are even and greater than 40000?

We must consider the first and last digits together.
Case 1: First digit is 4. Last digit must be 2 or 6 (2 choices).
\[ \quad \text{Middle 3 digits: } 3! = 6 \text{ ways. Total} = 1 \times 2 \times 6 = 12. \]
Case 2: First digit is 5. Last digit can be 2, 4, or 6 (3 choices).
\[ \quad \text{Middle 3 digits: } 3! = 6 \text{ ways. Total} = 1 \times 3 \times 6 = 18. \]
Case 3: First digit is 6. Last digit must be 2 or 4 (2 choices).
\[ \quad \text{Middle 3 digits: } 3! = 6 \text{ ways. Total} = 1 \times 2 \times 6 = 12. \]
Total = 12 + 18 + 12 = 42

6. Three girls and two boys are to be seated in a row. Find the number of different ways that this can be done if

6a. the girls and boys sit alternately

For 3 girls and 2 boys to alternate, the pattern must be G B G B G.
Arrange the 3 girls in the 3 'G' slots: 3! = 6 ways.
Arrange the 2 boys in the 2 'B' slots: 2! = 2 ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 6 \times 2 = 12 \]

6b. a girl sits at each end of the row

\[ \text{Pattern: G _ _ _ G} \]
\[ \text{Choose 2 girls for the ends from the 3 available: } ^3P_2 = 3 \times 2 = 6 \text{ ways.} \]
The remaining 3 people (1 girl, 2 boys) can be arranged in the middle 3 slots in 3! = 6 ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 6 \times 6 = 36 \]

6c. the girls sit together and the boys sit together

Treat the 3 girls as one block and the 2 boys as one block.
Arrange the 2 blocks: 2! = 2 ways (GB or BG).
Arrange the girls within their block: 3! = 6 ways.
Arrange the boys within their block: 2! = 2 ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 2 \times 6 \times 2 = 24 \]

7. Based on the word ORANGE:

7a. Find the number of different arrangements of the letters in the word ORANGE.

ORANGE has 6 distinct letters.
Number of arrangements = 6!
= 720

7b. Find the number of arrangements that begin with the letter O.

Fix O in the first position.
Arrange the remaining 5 letters in the remaining 5 positions: 5!
= 120

7c. Find the number of arrangements that have the letter O at one end and the letter E at the other end.

\[ \text{The ends can be (O _ _ _ _ E) or (E _ _ _ _ O): 2 ways.} \]
Arrange the remaining 4 letters in the middle 4 positions: 4! = 24 ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 2 \times 24 = 48 \]

8. Six-digit numbers are made using digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 without repetition. Assume that a number cannot begin with 0.

8a. Find the number of different six-digit numbers which can be made.

First digit cannot be 0 (5 choices: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).
The remaining 5 digits are chosen from the remaining 5 options and arranged: 5! ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 5 \times 5! = 5 \times 120 = 600 \]

8b. How many of the six-digit numbers in part a are even?

Last digit must be 0, 2, or 4. We must split into cases based on the last digit.
Case 1: Last digit is 0.
\[ \quad \text{First digit has 5 choices (1-5). Remaining 4 digits: } 4! \text{ ways. Total} = 5 \times 24 = 120. \]
Case 2: Last digit is 2 or 4 (2 choices).
\[ \quad \text{First digit cannot be 0 or the last digit (4 choices). Remaining 4 digits: } 4! \text{ ways. Total} = 2 \times 4 \times 24 = 192. \]
Total even numbers = 120 + 192 = 312

9. Six girls and two boys are to be seated in a row. Find the number of ways that this can be done if the two boys must have exactly four girls seated between them.

9. Arrangement with exactly four girls between the boys

The boys must be separated by 4 girls, so the pattern is B G G G G B.
Since there are 8 seats total, the boys can occupy the following position pairs:
(1, 6), (2, 7), or (3, 8). (3 possible patterns)
Arrange the 2 boys in their chosen 2 positions: 2! = 2 ways.
Arrange the 6 girls in the remaining 6 positions: 6! = 720 ways.
\[ \text{Total} = 3 \times 2 \times 720 = 4320 \]
