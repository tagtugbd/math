10.4: Combinations

Selections where order does not matter

Learn how to calculate the number of ways to select items from a group using the combination formula. This lesson covers basic combinations, multi-category selections, selections with constraints (like 'at least' or 'no more than'), and complementary counting methods.

45°

1. Without using a calculator, find the value of each of the following, and then use the ^nC_r key on your calculator to check your answers.

1a. \( {}^5C_1 \)

\[ \text{Formula: } {}^nC_r = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \]
\[ {}^5C_1 = \frac{5!}{1!(5-1)!} = \frac{5!}{1!4!} \]
= 5

1b. \( {}^6C_3 \)

\[ {}^6C_3 = \frac{6!}{3!(6-3)!} = \frac{6!}{3!3!} \]
\[ = \frac{6 \times 5 \times 4}{3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 20

1c. \( {}^4C_4 \)

\[ {}^4C_4 = \frac{4!}{4!(4-4)!} = \frac{4!}{4!0!} \]
Since 0! = 1, this is 1.

1d. \( {}^8C_4 \)

\[ {}^8C_4 = \frac{8!}{4!4!} \]
\[ = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6 \times 5}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 70

1e. \( {}^5C_5 \)

\[ {}^5C_5 = \frac{5!}{5!0!} \]
= 1

1f. \( {}^7C_4 \)

\[ {}^7C_4 = \frac{7!}{4!3!} \]
\[ = \frac{7 \times 6 \times 5}{3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 35

2. Show that {}^8C_3 = {}^8C_5.

2. \( \text{Prove } {}^8C_3 = {}^8C_5 \)

\[ \text{LHS} = {}^8C_3 = \frac{8!}{3!5!} \]
\[ \text{RHS} = {}^8C_5 = \frac{8!}{5!3!} \]
Since 3!5! = 5!3!, the expressions are identical.
\[ \text{Therefore } {}^8C_3 = {}^8C_5 \text{ (This illustrates the property } {}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n-r}\text{).} \]

3. How many different ways are there of selecting:

3a. 3 photographs from 10 photographs

Order does not matter, so we use combinations.
\[ ^{10}C_3 = \frac{10!}{3!7!} \]
\[ = \frac{10 \times 9 \times 8}{3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 120

3b. 5 books from 7 books

\[ ^{7}C_5 = \frac{7!}{5!2!} \]
\[ = \frac{7 \times 6}{2 \times 1} \]
= 21

3c. a team of 11 footballers from 14 footballers

\[ ^{14}C_{11} = ^{14}C_3 \quad (\text{since } {}^nC_r = {}^nC_{n-r}\text{)} \]
\[ = \frac{14 \times 13 \times 12}{3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 364

4. How many different combinations of 3 letters can be chosen from the letters P, Q, R, S, T?

4. \( {}^5C_3 \)

Choose 3 from 5 distinct letters.
\[ ^{5}C_3 = \frac{5!}{3!2!} = \frac{5 \times 4}{2 \times 1} \]
= 10

5. The diagram shows 2 different boxes, A and B. 8 different toys are to be placed in the boxes. Find the number of ways in which the 8 toys can be placed in the boxes so that 5 toys are in box A and 3 toys are in box B.

5. Placing 5 in A and 3 in B

We simply need to choose which 5 toys go into box A. The remaining 3 will automatically go to box B.
\[ \text{Number of ways} = {}^8C_5 \]
\[ = \frac{8!}{5!3!} = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6}{3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 56

6. 4 pencils and 3 pens are to be selected from a collection of 8 pencils and 5 pens. Find the number of different selections that can be made.

6. Selecting 4 pencils from 8 and 3 pens from 5

\[ \text{Choose 4 pencils from 8: } {}^8C_4 \]
\[ \text{Choose 3 pens from 5: } {}^5C_3 \]
Multiply the independent choices:
\[ ^{8}C_4 \times {}^5C_3 = 70 \times 10 \]
= 700

7. Four of the letters of the word PAINTBRUSH are selected at random. Find the number of different combinations if:

7a. there is no restriction on the letters selected

PAINTBRUSH has 10 distinct letters.
\[ \text{Choose 4 from 10: } {}^{10}C_4 \]
\[ = \frac{10!}{4!6!} = 210 \]

7b. the letter T must be selected

Fix T as one of the choices.
We need to choose the remaining 3 letters from the other 9 letters.
\[ ^{9}C_3 = \frac{9!}{3!6!} = \frac{9 \times 8 \times 7}{3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 84

8. A test consists of 30 questions. Each answer is either correct or incorrect. Find the number of different ways in which it is possible to answer:

8a. exactly 10 questions correctly

We need to choose which 10 questions (out of 30) are answered correctly.
\[ ^{30}C_{10} = 30045015 \]

8b. exactly 25 questions correctly

We need to choose which 25 questions are answered correctly.
\[ ^{30}C_{25} = ^{30}C_5 \quad (\text{choosing 25 correct is the same as choosing 5 incorrect}) \]
\[ = \frac{30 \times 29 \times 28 \times 27 \times 26}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} \]
= 142506

9. An athletics club has 10 long distance runners, 8 sprinters and 5 jumpers. A team of 3 long distance runners, 5 sprinters and 2 jumpers is to be selected. Find the number of ways in which the team can be selected.

9. Multi-category selection

\[ \text{Choose 3 runners from 10: } {}^{10}C_3 = 120 \]
\[ \text{Choose 5 sprinters from 8: } {}^8C_5 = 56 \]
\[ \text{Choose 2 jumpers from 5: } {}^5C_2 = 10 \]
Multiply the choices together:
\[ 120 \times 56 \times 10 = 67200 \]

10. A team of 5 members is to be chosen from 5 men and 3 women. Find the number of different teams that can be chosen:

10a. if there are no restrictions

Choose 5 from the total 8 people.
\[ ^{8}C_5 = {}^8C_3 \]
= 56

10b. that consist of 3 men and 2 women

\[ \text{Choose 3 men from 5: } {}^5C_3 = 10 \]
\[ \text{Choose 2 women from 3: } {}^3C_2 = 3 \]
\[ \text{Total} = 10 \times 3 = 30 \]

10c. that consist of no more than 1 woman

This means exactly 0 women OR exactly 1 woman.
\[ \text{Case 1 (0 women, 5 men): } {}^5C_5 \times {}^3C_0 = 1 \times 1 = 1 \]
\[ \text{Case 2 (1 woman, 4 men): } {}^5C_4 \times {}^3C_1 = 5 \times 3 = 15 \]
Total = 1 + 15 = 16

11. A committee of 5 people is to be chosen from 6 women and 7 men. Find the number of different committees that can be chosen:

11a. if there are no restrictions

Choose 5 from the total 13 people.
\[ ^{13}C_5 = \frac{13!}{5!8!} \]
= 1287

11b. if there are more men than women

Possible compositions (Men, Women): (5,0), (4,1), (3,2).
\[ \text{Case (5,0): } {}^7C_5 \times {}^6C_0 = 21 \times 1 = 21 \]
\[ \text{Case (4,1): } {}^7C_4 \times {}^6C_1 = 35 \times 6 = 210 \]
\[ \text{Case (3,2): } {}^7C_3 \times {}^6C_2 = 35 \times 15 = 525 \]
Total = 21 + 210 + 525 = 756

12. A committee of 6 people is to be chosen from 6 men and 7 women. The committee must contain at least 1 man. Find the number of different committees that can be formed.

12. At least 1 man

Use complementary counting: Total - Committees with 0 men.
\[ \text{Total possible committees (choose 6 from 13): } {}^{13}C_6 = 1716 \]
\[ \text{Committees with 0 men (choose 6 women from 7): } {}^7C_6 = 7 \]
Committees with at least 1 man = 1716 - 7 = 1709

13. A school committee of 5 people is to be selected from a group of 4 teachers and 7 students. Find the number of different ways that the committee can be selected if:

13a. there are no restrictions

Choose 5 from the total 11 people.
\[ ^{11}C_5 = \frac{11!}{5!6!} \]
= 462

13b. there must be at least 1 teacher and there must be more students than teachers

Since the committee has 5 people, and students > teachers, possible (Student, Teacher) splits are (3,2), (4,1), (5,0).
The condition 'at least 1 teacher' eliminates (5,0).
\[ \text{Case (3 Students, 2 Teachers): } {}^7C_3 \times {}^4C_2 = 35 \times 6 = 210 \]
\[ \text{Case (4 Students, 1 Teacher): } {}^7C_4 \times {}^4C_1 = 35 \times 4 = 140 \]
Total = 210 + 140 = 350

14. A test consists of 10 different questions. 4 of the questions are on trigonometry and 6 questions are on algebra. Students are asked to answer 8 questions. Find the number of ways in which students can select 8 questions if there are:

14a. no restrictions

Choose 8 from the total 10 questions.
\[ ^{10}C_8 = {}^{10}C_2 \]
\[ = \frac{10 \times 9}{2} = 45 \]

14b. at least 4 algebra questions

Total questions = 8. Number of Algebra (A) questions must be \ge 4. Max A = 6.
Possible (Algebra, Trig) splits: (4,4), (5,3), (6,2).
\[ \text{Case (4,4): } {}^6C_4 \times {}^4C_4 = 15 \times 1 = 15 \]
\[ \text{Case (5,3): } {}^6C_5 \times {}^4C_3 = 6 \times 4 = 24 \]
\[ \text{Case (6,2): } {}^6C_6 \times {}^4C_2 = 1 \times 6 = 6 \]
Total = 15 + 24 + 6 = 45

15. Rafiu has downloaded 10 movies to his smartphone. 4 of the movies are comedies, 3 are thrillers and 3 are science fiction. He is going to select 5 of these movies. Find the number of ways he can make his selection if:

15a. there are no restrictions

Choose 5 movies from the total 10.
\[ ^{10}C_5 = \frac{10!}{5!5!} \]
= 252

15b. his selection must contain his favourite thriller movie

Fix the favourite thriller (1 choice).
Choose the remaining 4 movies from the remaining 9 movies.
\[ ^{9}C_4 = \frac{9!}{4!5!} \]
= 126

15c. his selection must contain at least 3 comedy movies

Possible splits: (3 comedies, 2 others) OR (4 comedies, 1 other).
\[ \text{Case 1 (3C, 2 Others): } {}^4C_3 \times {}^6C_2 = 4 \times 15 = 60 \]
\[ \text{Case 2 (4C, 1 Other): } {}^4C_4 \times {}^6C_1 = 1 \times 6 = 6 \]
Total = 60 + 6 = 66

16. In a group of 15 entertainers, there are 6 singers, 5 guitarists and 4 comedians. A show is to be given by 6 of these entertainers. In the show, there must be at least 1 guitarist and 1 comedian. There must also be more singers than guitarists. Find the number of ways that the 6 entertainers can be selected.

16. Complex conditions

Let S = Singers, G = Guitarists, C = Comedians. Total = 6.
Conditions: G \ge 1, C \ge 1, S > G.
List valid combinations (S, G, C) and calculate:
If C=1: S+G=5 \implies (3,2,1) or (4,1,1).
\[ \quad (3,2,1): {}^6C_3 \times {}^5C_2 \times {}^4C_1 = 20 \times 10 \times 4 = 800 \]
\[ \quad (4,1,1): {}^6C_4 \times {}^5C_1 \times {}^4C_1 = 15 \times 5 \times 4 = 300 \]
If C=2: S+G=4 \implies (3,1,2).
\[ \quad (3,1,2): {}^6C_3 \times {}^5C_1 \times {}^4C_2 = 20 \times 5 \times 6 = 600 \]
If C=3: S+G=3 \implies (2,1,3).
\[ \quad (2,1,3): {}^6C_2 \times {}^5C_1 \times {}^4C_3 = 15 \times 5 \times 4 = 300 \]
Total = 800 + 300 + 600 + 300 = 2000

17. Given that 45 × {}^{n}C_4 = (n + 1) × {}^{n+1}C_5, find the value of n.

17. Solve for n

\[ \text{Recall the identity: } {}^{n+1}C_5 = \frac{n+1}{5} \times {}^nC_4 \]
Substitute into the equation:
\[ 45 \times {}^nC_4 = (n+1) \times \left( \frac{n+1}{5} \times {}^nC_4 \right) \]
\[ \text{Divide both sides by } {}^nC_4 \text{ (assuming } n \ge 4\text{):} \]
\[ 45 = \frac{(n+1)^2}{5} \]
\[ 225 = (n+1)^2 \]
Since n > 0, take the positive square root:
n+1 = 15
n = 14
